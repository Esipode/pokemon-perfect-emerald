## Okidogi — The Retaliatory Brawler Pokémon

Hitting Okidogi hard doesn't wear it down — it makes it angrier, and an angry Okidogi hits harder
and moves faster. The way through is provoking it into a full-blown rage, then punishing the
collapse that follows.

### RAGE — 0 to 6, both directions
Okidogi gets hurt and it climbs. It lands its own hit and it falls. Getting its Attack lowered, or
staying poisoned, also drives it up. The higher it climbs, the harder its own attacks hit.

### Phase 1 — 100% → 50%
Rage rises and falls with the exchange of hits. Lower its Attack stat and it snaps back angrier for
the trouble. A poisoned Pokémon feeds Rage every turn it stays poisoned.

### Phase 2 — Unchained (≤50% HP)
Okidogi speeds up and starts chaining a three-hit combo: a hard opening blow, a follow-up, then a
heavier finisher. Break its Defense or Special Defense, or land any status on it, mid-combo and the
whole sequence collapses — punishing that reset costs it a chunk of its own Rage.

### Phase 3 — Berserker (≤15% HP, the catch window opens at ≤10%)
Rage is pinned at maximum and its defenses drop hard. Every attack that whiffs bleeds a huge amount
of Rage back out — that's the bait. Land a hit while Rage is still maxed and everything it's built
up detonates in one devastating Collision Course, then the cycle starts over from empty. Poké Balls
open at 10% HP.

### What actually beats it
Provoke Rage to maximum on purpose, then either dodge/block its next swing to drain it (the safe
route) or eat one big hit to trigger Collision Course and immediately punish the wide-open defenses
that follow. Breaking the Unchained combo with a status move or a Defense/Special Defense drop is
free value.

### What does not work
Grinding it down slowly with steady damage just keeps its Attack climbing. Lowering its own Attack
stat backfires — it reads as disrespect and gets angrier for it.
