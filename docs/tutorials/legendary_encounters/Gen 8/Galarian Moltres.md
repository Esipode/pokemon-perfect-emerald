## Galarian Moltres — The Malevolent Pokémon

A vengeful spirit wrapped in dark flame. It doesn't just fight you — it learns to fear you less and
you fear it more, and the fight tilts with every step of that trade.

### DREAD — 0 (calm) to 5 (DARK AURA)

Everything that spooks your side pushes it up; everything that hurts Moltres' pride pulls it down.

**Raises Dread:**
- Getting hit by a Dark-type move, or any other damaging move
- A Pokémon of yours fainting (a bigger jump than a normal hit)
- Switching in a Pokémon that isn't Fighting/Fairy/Steel/Dark
- Your active Pokémon being statused

**Lowers Dread:**
- Landing a Rock, Electric, or Fairy move on Moltres — its own weaknesses
- Switching in a Fighting, Fairy, Steel, or Dark type

The higher Dread climbs, the harder Moltres hits (+1 Attack/Sp. Atk at Dread 2+), the more it shrugs
off (damage reduction rises at Dread 3+), and at Dread 5 it starts landing critical hits and DARK
AURA locks on — you'll be told when the aura rises or lifts.

### Phase 0 — Calm (100%–51%)

Dread moves freely both ways. Every turn Dread sits at 2 or higher, Moltres feeds — draining your HP
and healing itself, harder the higher Dread is.

### Phase 1 — Nightmare (50%–16%)

Same Dread rules, plus: switching a new Pokémon in risks a Nightmare mark for a few turns — Heal
Block, an accuracy drop, or a slow HP drain. You'll be told when a mark lands and when it fades.

### Phase 2 — Terror (≤15%, the catch window)

Dread is forced to 5 and DARK AURA locks on immediately. Grind it back to 0 with Courage (weakness
hits, resistant switches) to shatter the aura — Moltres' Defense drops hard when it breaks. Poké
Balls unlock once Moltres is at 10% HP or below.

### What actually beats it

Lead with something Rock/Electric/Fairy, or a Fighting/Fairy/Steel/Dark type, and keep Dread low the
whole fight — a calm Moltres hits like a normal legendary. Don't panic-switch after a faint; that's
the single biggest Dread spike in the fight.

### What does not work

Switching often "to find a good matchup" backfires — every switch that isn't into a resistant type
feeds Dread. Status moves don't help either; a statused mon keeps feeding Dread every turn it's
active.
