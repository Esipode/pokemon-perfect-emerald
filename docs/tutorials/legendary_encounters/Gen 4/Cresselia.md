## Cresselia — The Lunar Guardian

Cresselia fights behind the usual legendary package: a heavy damage reduction, immunity to OHKO,
fixed-damage, HP-swap and shared-KO moves, incoming type effectiveness capped at 2x, and flat Toxic
damage. It also **cannot be taken below 1 HP** for the whole fight, so the catch window at the end is
guaranteed no matter how the fight goes.

It keeps its own ability, **Levitate** — which matters more than it looks like it should.

Every other legendary in the set asks how hard you can hit. Cresselia asks something gentler, and the
answer is worse for you than fighting back would have been.

> **You have wandered into someone else's dream, and the dream would rather you stayed.**

### THE DREAM

One hidden number runs the whole battle, and it moves the opposite way you'd expect. It starts low
and climbs every time you are *kind*.

```
   WAKING   MOONLIT    LUCID    DEEP DREAM   REVERIE   PERFECT DREAM
     0         1          2          3           4            5
   guard 82  guard 84  guard 89   guard 92    guard 93     guard 95
```

Nothing on screen ever shows you this number. What it does show you is the moonlight, and the
moonlight describes it honestly, every step of the way — a line prints on every real change, in
either direction, and a short line keeps running underneath it every other turn for as long as
there's something to say.

### What moves it

| Deepens the dream (+) | Wakes it (−) |
| --- | --- |
| A quiet turn — Cresselia took under 2% of its max HP | Cresselia took 6% or more of its max HP this turn (overrides everything else) |
| You use a status-category move | Refusing the bargain (below) |
| Your Pokémon recovers 8% or more of its max HP | |
| A dream construct protects Cresselia (see below) | |
| The dream cures a status off Cresselia | |
| Accepting the bargain (below) | |

At most one step moves per turn. A turn where you hit hard is a turn the dream loses, whatever else
happened in it.

**The dream reads repetition, though.** Land the same one of Cresselia's three weakness types (Bug,
Ghost, or Dark) two turns running and the dream deepens a step on its own, on top of whatever the
turn's damage already did — so a heavy hit that repeats an angle of attack doesn't reliably keep the
dream pinned low the way varying your approach would. *"The dream has already learned to expect
that."*

### The trap

A careful, defensive, chip-damage game — exactly what a heavily damage-reduced wall invites — pins
the dial at 5 and gets tucked in behind a 95% guard, healed a little every turn, for its trouble.
Committing to real pressure holds the dial at 0-1 and fights something with a guard as soft as any
legendary in the set. The same attack lands roughly four times as hard at Waking as it does at
Perfect Dream.

### The dream's shelter

From **Lucid (Dream 2)** on, the world starts refusing harm — Misty Terrain, plus a Safeguard on
Cresselia's own side. **This immunity is earned by the meter, not innate.** Put Cresselia to sleep or
poison it while the dream is shallow and it works completely — but losing turns to a status is quiet,
quiet deepens the dream, and a deep-enough dream cures itself. The abuse buys you exactly the thing
that ends it.

From **Reverie (Dream 4)** on, Mist joins the field — Intimidate and stat-drop strategies stop
working. From **Perfect Dream (5)**, Aurora Veil goes up on top of everything else. None of it needs
hail; the dream raises it directly.

### Lucid Dreaming

From Dream 2 on, every so often Cresselia offers you something:

> *"Cresselia offers you something gentle, and asks nothing in return but that you take it.
> ACCEPT THE DREAM?"*

**Accept**, and the dream deepens a step, and you get one of three gifts at random:

| Gift | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| **Rest** | Your Pokémon is healed 35% and cured of its status |
| **Courage** | Your Pokémon's Attack and Sp. Atk both rise two stages |
| **Shelter** | Reflect and Light Screen go up on your side for 5 turns |

**Courage is the trap inside the trap.** The gift that best equips you to fight your way out is the
gift that pulls you one step deeper in.

**Resist**, and nothing happens for you — but the dream loses a step. It's the only instant way to
push it back besides raw damage, and it's free.

### Dream constructs

From Dream 3 on, Cresselia occasionally shelters behind a second shape rising out of the moonlight.
This is a real Protect — Feint, never-miss moves, and contact-punishing abilities all behave exactly
as they would against the real thing. Swing into it anyway and the construct hits back.

### Lucid Nightmare — 50%

*"Cresselia's dream begins to fracture."*

The dream stops being entirely under Cresselia's own control. Every third turn it lurches, one of
three ways, roughly equal odds:

| Lurch | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| The moonlight grows warm | Cresselia heals 8%, you heal 5%, the dream deepens a step |
| The dream distorts | Every screen and hazard on the field — yours **and** Cresselia's own shelter — is torn down together, and the dream loses a step |
| Cresselia restores the dream | Its status is cured, its stat drops washed away, the dream deepens a step |

Two out of three feed the dream, but the distortion is the only thing in the whole fight that strips
Cresselia's own shelter — worth watching for.

Aurora Beam gives way to **Future Sight** here.

### Eternal Dream — 25%

*"Cresselia's eyes begin to glow. It desperately clings to its dream."*

The meter stops moving — pinned at Perfect Dream in every way but the guard, which settles at **93**
instead of 95. From here Cresselia heals 2% every turn, continuously cures its own status and washes
away its own stat drops, and forms a construct every other turn instead of every third. Calm Mind
gives way to **Moonlight**, and the AI will use it.

**The break.** Land a **Bug-, Ghost-, or Dark-type move** — exactly the things Cresselia exists to
keep away — and:

> *"Your Pokémon awakens from the dream!"*

The guard drops to **82** for three turns, Cresselia loses 1 Sp. Def stage and its next action, and
the passive heal stops cold. It's repeatable — the last quarter of the fight is a race between the
closing window and a boss trying to heal back past you.

No coverage in those three types? A **very heavy hit** (roughly 6% or more of Cresselia's max HP in
one turn) breaks the dream too, while the window is shut. It's a worse door on purpose — guard 93
makes it a real ask — but it exists, so nothing here is a hard wall.

### Weakened — 10%

Balls unlock. This window is **identical on every path** — how you fought never decides *whether* you
get to catch it, only how the fight along the way felt. Every mechanic above shuts off, Cresselia's
status is cured, and its guard resets to a plain catch-window value.

### Its moves

`Psychic` · `Ice Beam` · `Calm Mind` (→ `Moonlight` at 25%) · `Aurora Beam` (→ `Future Sight` at 50%)

All legal Cresselia moves, played by the smart trainer AI. 70 Attack / 75 Sp. Atk means it will never
race you down — this fight is long because it is *hard to hurt*, not because it hits hard.

### If you take nothing else away

- The dream is not decoration. It is Cresselia's damage output, backwards from every other legendary.
- Playing it safe is the trap. Pressure — real, sustained pressure — is what keeps this fight short.
- Status works on a shallow dream. It stops working the moment the dream gets deep enough to matter.
- Leaning on one weakness type over and over is read. Vary your angle of attack to keep it honest.
- You cannot lose the catch window. You can absolutely make the fight to get there a long one.
